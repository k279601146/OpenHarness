# Brand Assets in Merchant Applications

Use when creating or applying a brand asset: product packaging, inserts, shop banners, identity concepts or mascot applications. A listing image does not require a new brand strategy.

## Reuse before redesign

Bind supplied/accepted logo artwork as the identity source. A brand name in a prompt is not an exact logo reference. Keep approved spelling, symbol, proportions and colors. A corner overlay follows the canvas; a printed mark follows its physical surface. Do not duplicate a corner logo when duplicating products.

Select applications from the merchant's request. No mandatory mockup count, full brand deck, HTML proposal, or new-logo stage is required. Independent applications of an existing logo can run together through [parallel-generation.md](parallel-generation.md).

## Make the application physically legible

| Application | Resolve before drafting | Prompt clause |
|---|---|---|
| Packaging/label | Actual package geometry, chosen face, artwork size/clearance, permitted copy | "Apply the supplied artwork to [surface], aligned with [face/edges], following its perspective while preserving the design and leaving [folds/seams/required copy] clear." |
| Printed insert/shop graphic | Content hierarchy and the actual brand asset | "Place the supplied logo at [position] with clear space from [nearby content]. Keep '[exact message]' and '[supporting copy]' in [hierarchy]." |
| Material mockup | Surface plus a plausible intended process | "Show the supplied mark as [ink print/emboss/engraving/embroidery] on [material and location]; preserve recognizable proportions and lettering while rendering [surface-specific texture]." |
| Mascot application | Accepted character identity, allowed pose/expression, relation to product | "Use the supplied character with unchanged [distinctive traits]. Change only [pose/expression] to [action], keeping [merchant product] recognizable and [contact] plausible." |

Paper printing, foil or embossing; metal engraving; and fabric embroidery/weaving suggest different depth and edge treatments. Select a process appropriate to the requested concept rather than defaulting to gold or an industry stereotype. A visual mockup is not proof of production feasibility. Very small lettering may require original artwork composition rather than generative redrawing.

## New identity only when requested

For a new logo, resolve name, application and available direction. Choose a wordmark when name recognition leads, a symbol when compact recognition matters, or a combination when both are needed. Useful mechanisms include a shared contour, negative space, or a letter modified by one relevant shape. Keep the name readable and the silhouette distinct at intended size; do not copy a sample brand's mark or force every concept into flat vector style.

Pattern: "Create a [wordmark/symbol/combination] for the exact name '[name]', intended for [application]. Combine [meaningful elements] using [shared contour/negative space/letter treatment]. Use [requested style and colors]; keep [lettering/silhouette requirement] clear at [use size]."

For a new mascot, translate requested traits into concrete choices of silhouette, proportion, expression and a distinctive feature. Choose a few visible decisions; do not write an archetype essay or treat shape associations as psychological facts. Example mechanism: a helpful character might have an open stance and attentive expression; it does not require a specific species or fixed head-to-body ratio.

Establish the actual chosen logo/character before dependent applications. If the user requests alternatives, keep each identity's applications separate. Do not mix two proposals or add an approval gate for an already accepted asset.

## Inspect and deliver honestly

Check exact lettering, proportions, symbol details and repeated identity across applications, then surface perspective, attachment, seams and legibility. A plausible texture must not deform the brand beyond recognition.

A raster logo or font-effect image is not an editable SVG or installable font. A mockup is not a print-ready dieline. Deliver the requested available artifact type and identify unsupported production deliverables. If exact logo preservation fails, use original artwork through an available composition method or report the mismatch.
