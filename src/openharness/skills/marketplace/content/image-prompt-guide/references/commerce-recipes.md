# Commerce Visual Recipes

Read only the entry relevant to the product. These are cleaned expression patterns, not product facts or mandatory templates. Use actual product references and confirmed copy. A generated illustration communicates a claim; it does not prove that claim.

## Audio: comfort and sound

- **Question:** How does it fit, or where would I use it?
- **Inputs:** Actual earbud/headphone shape and supported wearing orientation; confirmed feature if describing one.
- **Expression:** A wearing close-up with believable ear contact, or a listening scene with restrained sound visualization. Reserve a clean area only if copy is needed.
- **Do not import:** A sample brand, microphone count, decibel reduction, internal speaker layers or app controls. An exploded view requires actual construction evidence. Abstract sound rings should read as illustration, not measured acoustic data.

## Tools and fixtures: contact and operation

- **Question:** Where does it attach, what does it act on, and how is it held?
- **Inputs:** Product view, mounting/working point and compatible surface or fastener; additional views when necessary.
- **Expression:** Show the functional interface: base supported by the counter, bit aligned with a fastener, clamp contacting its workpiece. For edits affecting these relationships, apply the structural assessment and acceptance criteria in [image-edits.md](image-edits.md).
- **Check:** Contact, orientation, gravity, clearance and scale. Keep the tool recognizable. Do not add lighting modules, torque settings or attachments from a reference example.

## Wearables and jewelry: product-led poses

- **Question:** What does it look like when worn?
- **Inputs:** Product identity and, when scale matters, actual dimensions or a reliable wearing reference.
- **Expression:** Choose a pose exposing the main item: ear for earrings, neckline for a necklace, wrist/hand for a watch or ring. Use one clear visual focus when several accessories appear.
- **Check:** Correct attachment, realistic scale, contact shadows, gravity and occlusion. A ring wraps around the finger; a hair clip holds hair; chains hang or rest on supported surfaces. Style does not justify changing the item or obstructing it with a model's face.
- **Boundary:** A pose reference provides pose, not an unrequested person's identity or extra inventory.

## Food and pet products: packaging and use

- **Question:** What product is it, who uses it, and how is it served?
- **Inputs:** Actual packaging and known contents; confirmed ingredients if illustrated.
- **Expression:** Keep the pack readable, show a plausible serving/use context, and add an animal only where it helps explain the product.
- **Do not import:** Ingredient spreads, pack variants, medical outcomes or testimonials from another brand. A pet beside a bowl does not establish nutritional efficacy or preference.

## Materials, dimensions and comparisons

- **Detail:** Crop or use a real close-up to reveal texture, stitching or a connector. Generate a new detail view only within what the evidence supports.
- **Dimensions:** Use supplied values and correct measurement endpoints. Use precise overlay typography when needed; never infer millimeters from a photograph alone.
- **Comparison:** Compare confirmed attributes on comparable scales and conditions. Without measured outcomes, use a truthful usage contrast rather than fabricated charts or before/after claims.

## Transformable and assembled products

- **Question:** How does it change between supported states, and what space does it use?
- **Inputs:** Actual views or instructions for each state, visible joints/supports, and verified measurements if shown.
- **Expression:** Pair compact/open or installed/uninstalled states using the same product identity. Show which parts move and where the assembly remains supported. A mechanism detail can explain the transition if the construction is evidenced.
- **Prompt clause:** "Show [verified states] of the supplied product in [paired views or sequence]. Keep [fixed parts] consistent; change [supported moving parts] through [known relationship]. Match '[state labels]' to their respective views. Preserve [hinges, support and clearances]."
- **Check:** Each pictured state is supported by the sources, moving parts remain attached where appropriate, and labels belong to the right view. Do not turn multiple photos into invented intermediate states, hidden rails or extra panels.

## Turn category knowledge into a prompt

For the selected entry, state the actual subject, its contact/attachment, what must remain visible and the specific evidence. For a wearing image, for example: "Show [actual item] worn at [body location], attached by [visible or supplied attachment], with [pose] exposing [feature]. Keep [size/shape/material] and natural [contact/occlusion/gravity]." For a tool: "Align [working end] with [confirmed target], with [support/grip] and [clearance] visible."

Fill from the actual product rather than copying a category's assumed anatomy, accessory count or feature list. A posed or stylized scene may change presentation; it cannot establish specifications.

## Adapting an unfamiliar category

Borrow an expression mechanism, such as contact close-up, setup sequence or contents layout. Replace all product-specific assumptions with current evidence. If the evidence is insufficient, choose a simpler view or ask for the particular missing fact; do not read every neighboring category or fill gaps from its specifications.
